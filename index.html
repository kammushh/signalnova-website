Built for your market.

One transmitter design. Three very different power budgets.

0.7 – 4.2 GHz

Terrestrial base stations

Macro and small-cell radios spend much of their power budget on the transmitter. Higher efficiency cuts energy cost and the cooling hardware around it.

  • Lower power draw per radio
  • Smaller heat sinks and lighter enclosures
  • One transmitter design across several frequency plans

2 – 8 GHz

Satellites and ground gateways

In orbit every watt is generated, stored and cooled at a cost. Efficient transmitters reduce solar and thermal load, and on the ground they cut gateway power and cooling.

  • Less supply power and heat per transmitted watt
  • Wide bandwidth for high-throughput links
  • Compact CMOS transmitters for large constellations

0.7 – 8 GHz

Drones and portable communication

Drones and portable radios live on battery power and tight size limits. Efficiency extends mission time, and wide tuning supports frequency agility.

  • Longer battery life for the same output power
  • Small, light radios with less thermal design
  • Broad tuning range for frequency agility

What you gain.

Efficiency decides how much supply power becomes signal. The rest becomes heat you have to remove.

Less heat. Less cooling.

More of the supply power leaves as signal, so there is less heat to remove and less cooling hardware around the radio.

Power, in CMOS.
